Fantastic Four is a refreshing entry to a franchise that is slowly losing it's footing but I'm not sure if it does nearly enough to revitalize the same sense of wonder and interest in the MCU that I had lost quite a while ago.

There is a lot to love in this movie, the setting does the movie a lot of favours, not only does the distinct time period and aesthetic instantly set it apart from other Marvel entries, it also helps ground the story in a world that feels fresh and self contained. The retro futuristic vibe is super stylish. It's just a backdrop but it's one so rich in detail and personality that it elevates everything happening in front of it. One of the best looking Marvel movies easily.

The score compliments the setting so well. The synth, the french horns, and trumpets and everything that Giacchino is doing feels perfectly tuned to the world the film builds. It evokes that pulpy, space age grandeur of exploration, discovery and wonder but it's also very much a fanfare, a celebration of our heroes. The main motif of FAN-TA-STIC-FOUR is just so... declarative? An announcement? For the lack of a better word? it's triumphant, it's cheesy, it's fantastic.

Otherwise, it's a movie with a cookie cutter plot that’s better in parts than as a whole. I absolutely loved how some sequences played out. Some, not so much. The movie also does a very poor job at showing the passage of time. I don't want to call this a bits and pieces movie, it's not entirely disjointed but the film does feel a little chopped, like there's a quarter movie missing somewhere. It feels more like a series of well executed snapshots than a fully realized, cohesive journey. It's filled with moments worth remembering strung together with little that invites you to relive the entire experience. That's just a really fancy of saying that I'm much more likely to watch individual scenes on YouTube than watch the entire movie as a whole when I want to revisit it.

I think my enjoyment of this movie was deeply tied to the history I already have with these characters. There’s a kind of emotional shorthand that fans of source material bring with them to the adaptations. We KNOW what the character speaks like. We KNOW the journeys they've been on. We KNOW the iconic dialogues and poses, and character dynamics that the film doesn't need to explain because we've already seen them play out. A simple glance carries decades of context. The film does a wonderful job at bringing the Reed Richards that I've known through pages to life that I can't help but feel a sense of familiarity whenever he's on screen. It feels like watching an old friend step out of the page. Even if he looks like Pedro Pascal. The writing carries, really. All the little moments with Ben and Johnny are everything I've ever asked for. The movie gets Reed. It gets Johnny. Vanessa Kirby as Sue is an easy stand out and is the definitive version of the character.

This emotional inheritance can both elevate and undermine the experience. Without that pre-existing connection I hold for these characters, I imagine the experience would have felt a lot more hollow but this shorthand is a double edged sword. Because I was simply left expecting more. Ben is simultaneously my favourite and my least favourite part of the movie. He's a tragic figure cloaked in stone whose strength is only matched by his humanity. But just as essential to who he is, is that big hearted, rough around the edges, Yancy Street charm. The film hints at the pathos, the burden of his transformation and the isolation it brings, it never explores them completely but it's something that I can forgive the movie for considering it's set 4 years later but in a lot of ways, it stops short of embracing the full spectrum of what makes Ben, Ben.

It leaves out the more eccentric edges of his personality, sands down the lovable cornball beneath the craggy exterior. His trademark Brooklyn accent is practically nonexistent, and with it goes the unique voice, both literally and figuratively that gives him so much personality. I felt a little pinch in my heart whenever Ben says "Sue" instead of "Suzie" or "Stretch" instead of "Strecho". He’s not just the sensitive bruiser of the group, he's more, he’s the comic relief, the sentimental glue, the guy who’ll punch out a god and then crack a joke about missing poker night. The film seems almost hesitant to lean into that, to let him be bigger than life in the way the best versions of him are. The movie barely does anything with him. It barely scrambles together something to give for Johnny which ends up feeling a little out of character but hey, at least it doesn't treat him as a dumb hothead caricature.

With all it's flaws and hesitation, Fantastic Four was still an enjoyable film for what it was, I walked out satisfied and with a smile on my face, something I haven't been able to say for MCU projects of late barring a very few.
