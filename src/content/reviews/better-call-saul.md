It only took a twenty second clip of a rather very tired Bob Odenkirk settling down in his incommodious office, checking his phone and realizing not a single soul had called him to make me go "Yeah, this show is peak". That was one of the first clips I saw of the show, forcefully fed by the Instagram algorithmic overlords, years before I properly got around to watching it but little did I know that this small moment would perfectly outline a theme that would run throughout the entire series. Jimmy's isolation and his subsequent evolution into Saul Goodman as an exercise in survival. The scene on its own is a silly little scene with a silly little punchline. Feeling the twinge of disappointment waking up to no notifications is an universal experience but behind the punchline is a glimpse into Jimmy's struggle to crave out a place in a world that seems to have no room for him. 



You start the show with a chuckle but it does not take too long before you find yourself in Jimmy's world where every decision is a tight ropewalk between ambition and morality. It's about survivability, it's about evolution and it's about the slow, slow death of one Jimmy McGill. 



Jimmy's battle with his identity and acceptance of it by the lawyer world is a central theme of the show that runs parallel with the conflict between societal expectations and personal transformation.



Piece by piece, the show builds a portrait of a man on the precipice of a total personal collapse. A man who will do whatever it takes to survive, to make it, even if it means bending and outright shredding the rules. Even if it means breaking bad. 


## In the Shadow of the Flock

At the beginning of the series, Jimmy is trying hard to leave behind his past as "Slippin' Jimmy" and earn his place among the respectable lawyers in Albuquerque. He works his way up from the mailroom at Hamlin, Hamlin & McGill (HHM), earns a law degree through legitimate means, earns a legitimate place in the lawyer world but has to fight to be seen as a legitimate lawyer. 

Chuck, his successful and respected brother, sees him as a conman and a troublemaker, unable to change his true nature. Chuck's judgment is rooted in a mix of narcissism, scepticism, and a rigid belief in the law's sanctity, leading him to undermine Jimmy's progress at every turn. The broader legal community mirrors this contempt Despite Jimmy's honest attempts to earn a place among them, his unconventional methods and background are met with suspicion and exclusion. This collective attitude toward Jimmy speaks of a tale that is as old as time: those who don't fit into established norms are often ostracized, reinforcing a cycle of rejection. The constant judgment, elitism, belittlement, and rejection become more than obstacles to Jimmy's success. They reinforce his identity as an outsider. And the more he is denied a place among them, the further he is pushed toward becoming the very person they always believed him to be.

![Better Call Saul S1E10](/gitforked/images/BCS/better-call-saul-season-1-episode-10-frame-00110.webp)

This systemic rejection sent a clear message to Jimmy. No matter how hard he tries to play by the rules, he will never be accepted. The constant scepticism and lack of trust forces him into a corner, where he realizes that there's only one way for any one for a man like him to make it in the world that punches him down regardless of his best efforts.

## 'S All Good Man

As Jimmy finds himself increasingly marginalized, he begins to question the value of playing by the rules in a world that won't accept him. This shift in perspective is analogous to existential philosophy, where individuals realize that societal constructs are arbitrary, leading to a sense of absurdity and disillusionment. Faced with an unjust system, Jimmy's mindset shifts from seeking acceptance to exploiting the system to his advantage. These ongoing rejections drove Jimmy to embrace a darker path, effectively saying, "If you see me as a con man regardless of what I do, you know what, then fuck you, I'll become the best con man you've ever seen." Leading him down a path where he embraces the very role they ascribe to him.

!['SallGoodMan'](/gitforked/images/BCS/SallGoodMan.png)

Saul Goodman was his way of reclaiming power in a system that consistently marginalized him. The persona of Saul allows him to navigate a world that rewards ruthlessness and creativity over honesty and integrity. When individuals are consistently viewed through a negative lens, they may eventually internalize those perceptions. In becoming what they say he is, Jimmy's journey becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy. The more he is treated as a con man, the more he adopts those traits, reinforcing the cycle of negative expectations. 



Jimmy's struggle to define himself in a world that seeks to pigeonhole him reflects the broader question of how much control we have over our own identities. His transformation into Saul Goodman is both a reaction to external pressures and an assertion of autonomy. He chooses to embrace the persona that provides him with success, even if it means sacrificing his own moral compass.



As Saul Goodman, Jimmy fully embraced a persona that was the antithesis of his earlier self. He became a character defined by slick suits, ties that don't match, unethical tactics, and a cavalier attitude toward the law. Saul was a carefully constructed façade, designed to thrive in the grey areas of the legal world and beyond. However, this new identity, while allowing him success, also came with a high cost.


## The Chimp, Chicanery and the Consequences. 


Enter Kim. When the world didn't accept Jimmy, the one person who did. Kim has been his confidante, partner, and moral compass, often grounding him and challenging him to be better. However, their relationship becomes strained as Jimmy becomes more entrenched in the Saul Goodman lifestyle. When Kim decides to leave Albuquerque and walk away from their relationship, it should've been a wake up call for Jimmy but Kim's departure becomes a catalyst for his descent deeper into the Saul persona. 

![Kim Wexler](/gitforked/images/BCS/EnterKim.png)

What was a professional strategy becomes a psychological defence mechanism, a way to distance himself from the rejection that he was all too familiar with but this time, it was by someone who stood by him even when the entire world stood against him. He descends further into the façade that allowed him to thrive, but at the cost of his own authenticity. The wolf's clothing provides protection, but it also imprisons him within a role that ultimately distances him from his values and relationships. The longer he wears this guise, the more he becomes trapped in the expectations and limitations of the wolf's role.



When the fallout from his involvement with Walter White and the broader Breaking Bad universe catches up with him, Saul goes into hiding, adopting a new identity as Gene Takavic in Omaha, Nebraska. The monotony and isolation of his new life force him to reflect on his past actions and the person he has become. A deliberate choice to avoid the legal and criminal entanglements that defined his time as Saul. However, Gene's retreat into obscurity does not bring him the peace he seeks. Instead, it serves as a constant reminder of the consequences of his actions and the person he once was. One thing leads to another and at a point, he faces a crucial decision: continue running or confront the consequences. He choses neither. He chooses to con his way out. He chooses to become Saul again. Despite his moments of introspection and desire for redemption, Jimmy remains tethered to his past as Saul Goodman but chicanery always pays.


![Gene Takavic](/gitforked/images/BCS/Gene.png)

## Jimmy's Greatest Con

Initially, after his capture, Saul Goodman proves once again his mastery of manipulation and legal maneuvers by striking a deal that would reduce his sentence from life imprisonment to a mere seven years. One of many cons that Jimmy has pulled from the days of being Slippin Jimmy to being Gene, perhaps one of his best but even still, his greatest con remains his illusion of self. 



Saul Goodman is a façade he constructed to survive and thrive in a world where traditional rules didn't favor him. This persona was his greatest con, a carefully crafted identity that allowed him to navigate the criminal underworld and protect himself from legal consequences and emotional turmoil. A coping mechanism and imprisonment of self. On a superficial level, what represented Jimmy's adaptation to a harsh legal world that judged him based on his past and denied him a fair chance became a shield against personal vulnerabilities, social rejection and the pain of losing the ones he loved.


![Alter](/gitforked/images/BCS/Alter.png)

By creating this alter ego, Jimmy erected a barrier between his inner vulnerabilities and the harsh realities of his world. Saul was his armor against rejection and failure. A confident, flamboyant persona who couldn't be hurt by criticism or failure because he didn't operate by the same rules as everyone else. However, this protective barrier also trapped Jimmy in a cycle of deceit, preventing him from confronting the emotional toll of his actions and the consequences of his decisions.



Over time, the Saul Goodman façade began to take over Jimmy's life, becoming not just a persona but an entire lifestyle. The more he relied on Saul to navigate the legal and personal worlds, the further he distanced himself from the honest, hopeful aspects of his original identity. This transformation was a cage: while it offered protection, it also limited his ability to change, grow, and reconnect with his true self.

## It's Showtime, Folks 





Having negotiated a remarkably lenient seven year sentence, having presented himself as the victim of Walter White and framing his involvement in the crimes as something he was merely swept into. He is prepared to walk away as Saul Goodman once again, ready to successfully talk his way out of facing the full weight of what he had done. But then, he confesses. 



A pivotal moment occurs when he learns that Kim Wexler, his former partner and the person he cared for most, confessed her role in Howard Hamlin's death and faced the consequences of her actions. Her decision to take responsibility confronts Jimmy with the possibility of another life, the one he could have lived had he ever found the courage to stop running from himself.


Love, as a driving force in Jimmy's journey, amplifies the existentialist theme of authenticity. Love challenges Jimmy to confront his own vulnerabilities and insecurities, pushing him to reject the superficiality of Saul Goodman and embrace the sincerity of Jimmy McGill. He'd rather spend his life in prison as Jimmy and be able to look at Kim in the eye than walk out after seven years and spend the rest of it free as Saul, alone. He could walk free, but freedom would mean continuing to live inside the lie he had constructed, knowing that Kim, the person whose opinion mattered most, would see through it.  



Recognizing the cage he had put himself in, Jimmy chooses to break free from the façade. Jimmy renounces the deal he secured, confessing his role in Walter White's criminal enterprise and his involvement in Chuck McGill's tragic death. This is Jimmy reclaiming his identity. For the first time in years, he chooses to exist without the protection of an alias, without a con to hide behind, and without the need to deceive himself or anyone else.


![It's Showtime](/gitforked/images/BCS/Showtime.png)

Jimmy McGill's journey ultimately becomes a testament to the possibility of moral reckoning in a world that often rewards those willing to abandon it.



It is a little funny, looking back. I was expecting Jimmy's transformation into Saul Goodman to be an exciting, cheer worthy moment but when it finally happened, there I was, mourning for Jimmy's death instead of cheering for Saul's arrival. We are conditioned to see transformation as progress, to assume that becoming more powerful, more confident, or more ruthless means becoming more of ourselves. But Jimmy's transformation is the inverse. Saul is everything Jimmy thinks he needs to be to survive, and almost everything he needs to be to forget who he is. The tragedy isn't that Jimmy becomes a different man. It's that he becomes extraordinarily good at pretending he isn't himself.


Perhaps the greatest testament to Better Call Saul is that we, the audience, stop calling him Saul altogether. We call him Jimmy. Despite the show being sold on the fast talking, morally slippery lawyer from Breaking Bad, yet after witnessing the life that existed behind that name, it is Jimmy McGill whom we mourn, it is Jimmy McGill whom we root for and it is Jimmy McGill's return whom we cherish.

And perhaps that is what Jimmy's story ultimately leaves us with. We cannot always undo what we have done. We cannot reclaim every relationship, heal every wound, or return to the person we simply were. But we are never entirely powerless over who we become next. There is always another choice to make, another opportunity to stop running, to take responsibility, to put down the mask and decide that the person we have been does not have to dictate the person we will be.

Because sometimes, after everything falls apart, after the lies have been told and the damage has been done, the most courageous thing you can do is choose to begin again.

It's all good, man.
