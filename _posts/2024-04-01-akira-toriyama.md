One of my very first conversations with my now good friend before I ever got to know him like I do now was when we talked about the ideas we had that stayed as ideas instead of ever taking proper form and I remember telling him that the best part about these stories is that they keep evolving. With time. With us. Akira Toriyama is a testament to artistic evolution. 

![Alt text]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/toriyama.png)
\
The man shifted gears from drawing gag comics to riffing on wuxia/pro wrestling, and did it effortlessly so well that he irreversibly altered at least three mediums for decades to come. All while staying a gag artist. 

![Alt text]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/gokugohan.png)

Other than being your GOAT's GOAT, other than establishing character archetypes and narratives that have become quintessential to the medium over the last few decades, Toriyama's influence far exceeds the boundaries of manga or any storytelling medium. Toriyama's ideas left an everlasting impact, not just influencing countless artists, writers and creators but also shaping the zeitgeist in the process. 

\
You could be dropped in a random country in South Africa and have nothing in common but I assure you, they will recognise a Goku tee. You might get out of a cartel abduction alive if you can quote Goku. Never before has a nation hosted a watch party just to see fictional characters duke it out, to see Goku, the alien mortal who surpassed the Gods to fight for the universal's survival in a multiversal free for all. And all that started with a blue haired teenager and a monkey tailed kid. 

![Alt text]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/theend.png)

To the man who sparked a creative revolution that resonates across borders, languages and across generations, to the man who taught many how to draw. To the man who helped me through soothadis. 

\
REST IN POWER, GOAT.
