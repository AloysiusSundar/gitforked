I like having a moral compass. It’s just inconvenient how it often points directly into a storm.

When someone turns toward the good, sincerely, awkwardly and earnestly. It rarely goes unnoticed. Not by others, not by the world, not even by the invisible. In Catholicism, and echoed in other spiritual traditions, there is a quiet belief that when a soul shifts course toward virtue, a strange resistance begins. Life, which once moved in predictable rhythms, suddenly grows heavy. Small hopes collapse. Old temptations return, now somehow stronger. What was once easy becomes inexplicably hard. 

\
To move toward honesty, towards God, or whatever one calls the source of goodness, is to provoke attention. Heaven sees, yes; but so does everything else. When you were sleeping, there was little need to stir the waters. You caused no trouble. But goodness is disruptive. Real change, the kind that pulls roots and disturbs the soil, is always loud.

\
The saints often spoke of this. Teresa of Ávila, for example, spoke candidly of the frustration and confusion. she had to face.  She was not a romantic. It was as though the act of drawing near to the sacred summoned opposition. This idea runs through the old stories, too. Christ himself was not tempted in the desert *before* he began his mission but *because* he began it. The desert came after the baptism, after the light and the dove and the voice. There’s something telling in that order. The darkness often waits just outside the threshold of the sacred. Maybe it’s just divine warfare. Maybe it’s the universe conspiring against you. Maybe it’s the Gods testing you. Maybe it’s the Devil tempting you. Maybe this great chaos is just a part of the plot for your narrative arc. Or maybe, sometimes, well, shit just happens.

\
When someone decides to change for the better, quit the poison, take responsibility, the world rarely rolls out a carpet for the good. In fact, it often spits them back. This has been interpreted spiritually in many religions but the dynamic is older than religion. It is psychological. It is political. It is human. 

\
A person asleep drifts easily; a person climbing a mountain feels the strain. People are systems. So are societies. And systems resist disruption. If you've been complicit in a broken machine and then pull the lever to stop it, the machine does not thank you. It groans and shakes. Sometimes it crushes. Because goodness is not neutral. It exposes. It challenges the established order, whether personal or political. Try to change yourself, and you will meet not just your bad habits, but all the invisible contracts that allowed them to exist. Try to speak honestly in a dishonest environment and watch how fast the room turns. Try being kind in a competitive one. You'll notice very quickly how little the system appreciates your newfound clarity. Institutions, whether corporate or familial or cultural, are built on stability. That stability often relies on a few shared lies and a collective decision not to poke at them. You, deciding to improve, are now poking.

\
Truth creates discomfort. It makes people defensive. Systems prefer stability to integrity. When someone tries to stop lying to themselves, the mind reacts. Shame shows up. Doubt shows up. Old stories come rushing in. Stories about why you’ll never change, why you’ll fail again. Resistance is not a sign that we are wrong; it is often a sign that we have finally become noticeable. There are patterns that do not want to let us go. Habits that have shaped us for years. It is about transformation. And transformation, by its nature, is uncomfortable. Birth is never quiet. It tears and stretches and bleeds. If the soul is to be reborn, it will feel some of that same ache. If your heart has been closed for years, to open it will take time. And pain.

\
Philosophically, we could say that good has a cost. Not in the sense of divine punishment, but in the simple mechanics of life. Every value you hold will be tested. If you claim to care about justice, you will be asked to act when it’s hard. If you claim to value truth, you will be tempted to lie when it’s convenient. In this sense, bad things don’t happen *because* you’re doing good. They happen to *verify* it.

\
There is also ego. When we try to become better, part of us mourns. The part that liked being liked. The part that felt safe. The part that didn’t want to look closely. And as it dies, it protests. Loudly. You can hear it in the inner monologue: “This is pointless. No one else is doing this. You’re making things harder for yourself.” And it's right. You are.

But that’s the point.

To become better is not to escape pain but to choose a better kind. A pain with meaning. A pain that shapes rather than shatters. A pain that strips away what is false so something more solid can stand in its place.

\
There’s a decent case to be made for randomness. Entropy doesn’t care about your moral journey. Sometimes things fall apart because they were never held together well in the first place. Sometimes you’re not being tested; sometimes the roof just leaks but the fact that suffering so often coincides with growth, even by accident, is still worth paying attention to. Even if it’s not some cosmic drama, it’s a kind of psychological one. Your old self, your environment, your comfort zones, they don’t go down without a scuffle. Every little change is a small revolution. And revolutions, however modest, tend to make a mess before they make anything better.

\
So yes, maybe it’s not hell noticing you. Maybe it isn’t because mercury is in retrograde but the resistance you feel when you try to do better, it’s real. It means you’re pushing against something. Whether it’s your own past, your social circle, or something more ancient, more unseen, the friction is real and it tells you that you’ve moved. And movement, even awkward, limping, reluctant movement, is the only real sign that you’re not stuck.

\
If bad things follow your attempt to grow, don’t be too quick to assign meaning but don’t ignore the pattern either. Sometimes, it really is just life being life. But other times, you’ve disturbed something. Something that was counting on your silence. And now it has to reckon with your noise. So yeah, this moral compass, it drags you into storms. It complicates things. Makes life messier than it has to be. But it also makes sure you’re not adrift. And in a world where it’s easy to get lost, where you can sell your soul in a thousand tiny, polite ways without ever noticing, it’s something to hold on to. Even if your grip hurts sometimes.
