There was a time, about a decade ago when I felt as though my life has turned meaningless. When I felt like my existence has ceased to provide any purpose of any sort. Ceased. It is a word I chose carefully because I didn’t always feel this way. It wasn’t a realization that my life was meaningless. It was the mourning of my purpose being robbed. Except it wasn’t robbed. I killed it. Due to my own frivolous ways. It’s been a decade and all it takes is a song that you listened to a lot from then to put you there again in an instant. To make you realize that maybe things hasn’t changed as much.

I am not the same man I was a decade ago but I am no better. I’d like to think I’ve grown, I’d like to think I’ve learnt from my mistakes, found clarity but to say I am better would be to say that I know what the fuck I’m doing. I don’t. I’m “spray and pray”-ing my life through.

Sure, I’ve changed. Time does that. Life does that. It chips away at you, wears down your edges. You run into situations, You meet new people, You pick up new scars, learn new tricks, bury old wounds. But better? Better is a word reserved for people who’ve turned their lives around in some measurable way, found new meaning. I’ve traded one set of fuck-ups for another. 

A decade ago, I was reckless in ways I can’t relate to now. In ways it makes me cringe now. I made decisions that were impulsive, selfish, sometimes cruel. But now? Now I make decisions that are calculated, careful, maybe even a little too cautious, but I’m still screwing up things just as ever. It’s just that the fuck-ups look different now. Maybe they’re less dramatic, less chaotic, but they still cut just as deep. Maybe I’m more wiser than I was, maybe I know more than I did but my actions still hurt just as much. 

Have I grown? Maybe. Have I learned? I’d like to think so. But am I better? I don’t know. Better implies I’m closer to something I’m proud of. I used to have this version of myself in my head, this version I made up after a certain fateful day in my life a decade ago, everything I did, every action I made, every word I said since had just been me trying to be more like him. The one who was always in control, the one who always knew the right move and always did what’s right, not out of any need of approval, not because he wanted to or because he’s overwhelmed with a sense of justice but simply because it was the right thing to do. Simply because it is just. It was me adopting a persona… a persona I conjured of myself. This facade helped me get through times I once thought I’d never make it out of but what happens when this facade is challenged?

This version of me seemed perfect. Was perfect. Untouchable. He had friends, but never got too close. He helped people, but never let them in. He kept distance and had it all figured out. I thought if I made all the right choices, if I shut down all the messy emotions and the impulses, I could be him. The guy in my head. This idealized, detached, this superior version of myself who was above all things that made life complicated. I rejected love. I rejected happiness. I didn’t need them. Or so I told myself. I don’t have anyone to help me? Fine, I don’t need anyone’s help. Except it was my own hand that pushed away the hands that offered help. I thought I was better off without the mess that needless emotions bring. But I’d soon find out that it’s easy to say “Eh,  I don’t really want to be happy” when happiness feels like a distant memory. When you haven’t felt it in so long that you forget what it feels like. I got good at pushing the emotions away. I got good at pushing people away. I convinced myself that not needing anyone or anything for that matter meant that I am independent, that I am in control. That I was safe. 

I changed the way I dress. I lost weight. I changed the way I spoke. I went by a new name. I made new friends. I turned cold.  I was a brand new person, someone who is better. someone who is more akin to the one I had in my head.

But life’s funny. It happened. After almost a decade, it did. Maybe it was the people around me Maybe it was just me. Maybe it was this realization that my life wasn’t crumbling away slowly anymore but somehow, despite my best efforts to the contrary, I found myself happy. Genuinely so. The kind of happiness that sneaks up on you when you least expect it to. Catches you off guard and wraps itself around you before you can even think to push it away. Before you even realize.

The facade was challenged. It started to crack. I wholeheartedly believed that if I was more like the guy in my head, I’d be untouchable. The guy who always tanked punches, the guy who always got back up. My entire existence was built upon this frankly problematic conjuration that wasn’t really all that sturdy to begin with and when you build your identity around a version of yourself that doesn’t even feel real, a version that wasn’t built on experiences, a version that is untested in real life, you’re bound to crumble under the weight of it.

Every time I failed to live up to him, it felt as if though this version of myself was standing beside me, looking at me judgingly, with a sharp glare of disappointment. Each time I let someone in, each time I made a decision based on emotions, each time I actively chose to do the thing that would make me ever so slightly happy than the ideal thing, I felt like I was betraying the guy in my head. This perfect, this superior version of myself. This version of myself who saved me from what was seemingly a bottomless pit. The bars that protected me from the threats outside became my cage.

It wasn’t too late before I realized that this perfect version of myself wasn’t so perfect after all. It wasn’t self awareness that I lacked, it was the courage to change. To change again. To turn heed from what was essentially my whole identity now. 

What happens when the facade is challenged? You see yourself for who you really are, and maybe that’s the hardest part. It’s this scary, frankly frightening realization that this person you’ve been trying to emulate was just never you in the first place. The guy in the head, he wasn’t a goal, he wasn’t the destination, he was an escape route. He was cold because he was afraid of feeling anything too deeply. He wasn’t confident, he was terrified of failure. He just pretended not to care.

What happens when this facade is challenged? It’s one thing to say that you don’t want happiness when you’ve been a living void because of depression and then equally numb because of anti-depressants. When you’ve forgotten what it means to genuinely smile, to feel joy, to let yourself hope for a future where you are happy. It’s easy then. It’s an entirely different ball game when you’ve held it in your hands. When you’ve tasted it and just like that, it gets ripped away. Just when you let yourself believe, for a moment that maybe, just maybe it isn’t so bad to feel something again. 

I was hurt. I was distraught. I could’ve doubled down. I could’ve said “SEE? I WAS RIGHT”. 

When you’ve finally let yourself feel happy and it’s gone, it’s the universe saying “yeah fuck this guy in particular”. All those years of rejecting it felt like a cruel joke. Like an especially distasteful joke you’d see on practical jokers or siricha pochu. The emptiness now was much worse than the emptiness that existed before. Because now you know. Because now you finally understood what it’s like. To be happy. In it’s own fucked up sense, it also felt validating. To the guy in the head. If I didn’t allow myself to be happy, if I had continued to reject it instead of rejecting the persona, I wouldn’t have been hurt. I wouldn’t have found myself weeping every night. The guy in my head, the one who’s been running the show before I took the wheel back from him, he was grinning at me from the corner saying “See? Told ya”. I wanted to double down. The gruesome pain, the miserable days and the endless nights, they felt like proof that I was right all along. Right to stay guarded, right to push people away. Right to push happiness away because happiness, as beautiful as it was in that fleeting moment, it felt like it was all a carefully choreographed set up. A trap. I let my guard down for a second, let myself hope and the world yanked it away. 

I wanted to double down. I could’ve doubled down. It felt almost safer to wallow in the misery. But once you’ve felt it. Once you’ve felt happiness, once you’ve had it stripped away from you, you start to realize just how much you’ve been lying to yourself. Doubling down might stop me from hurting in the future but it also would’ve meant giving up on something I didn’t realize I wanted. No. Something I needed. Something that deep down, I was still chasing even if I had told myself I wasn’t. 

I could’ve kept my heart in ice, built back the wall taller but now that I had felt it, even for brief moments, I knew what was possible. And as much as it hurt to lose it, I couldn’t forget what it felt like to be alive. For once, Truly alive. I could’ve doubled down but for the first time, I hesitated. And that hesitation meant something. Maybe it meant that I wasn’t as cold as I thought. 

I still haven’t let go of this version in my head, I would be lying if I said I know how to. If I said I wholeheartedly want to. He seems unkillable, impossible to get rid of, he stays in a corner of my head, always taunting, still acting as my moral compass at times but I’ve given up on chasing him and without this version, or any version to chase, I’m left with the raw, unpolished truth. I’m not as calculated or as in control I thought I was. I feel things deeply, I care too much. I want to share too much. I’m far more flawed, far more human and that’s okay. I think. 

Every day is a chance to start fresh. I try every day. I keep failing. Each day, each second chance feels like a blank, fresh state, a new start. Each one a fresh opportunity to rewrite the narrative.

I’m not one to lack self awareness. There’s no ignorance to hide behind. I can see the trainwreck happening in slow motion. I’m in the middle of it. I have the power to stop it. “Next day”, I tell myself but how many next days before you run out of it. You try to be accountable. You try to make amends. You promise that the next time will be different, and deep down, you even convince yourself it will be. But history’s a twisted little bitch, and she loves to repeat herself. 

Every day is a chance to start fresh. I try everyday. I keep failing. I try everyday regardless. Maybe there’s something in that persistence, something worth clinging to. Hope. Even when it feels futile. Even when it feels like I’m destined to trip over my own feet. I cling on to hope.

Life is unpredictable. You can never account for everything. You can never be too calculative. Life is hard. It’s cruel. It’s uncaring. It’s beautiful. There’s no algorithm for pain or joy or the messy intersections of both. Life is confusing. It doesn’t give two shits about your plans or your dreams. Life goes on, indifferent to your struggles, indifferent to your pain, indifferent to your euphoria, to the moments that leave you breathless in despair. And yet, somehow, somewhere amidst all this, there’s a beauty to be found. There’s a certain sense of pure, raw, unfiltered beauty in the way the sun breaks through the clouds after a storm, the way raindrops fall off a petal, the way a laugh can light up a gloomy room. The way you only miss the laugh when it’s not there anymore. There’s beauty in all of this. The little moments. It’s easy to focus on your failure, to let them define you, to wallow in the bitterness of how much you’ve lost. But every time you rise again, you’re proving something powerful. You’re telling the universe, “’m not fucking staying down. I’m not letting these failures define me”. Each fuck up becomes a badge of honor. A proof to the unwillingness to be crushed by the weight of it all. A stubborn spark that refuses to be extinguished. 

The irony isn’t lost on me. I’ve spent so much of my life clinging to the idea that tomorrow would bring a new beginning, that I could always hit reset and start over. As I have in the past. I’ve gotten out of situations I shouldn’t have gotten out of, I’ve outsmarted fate with my spite. With my optimism but eventually the well runs dry and the truth starts to sink in; maybe there are only so many times you can give yourself a pass before the universe stops caring. Maybe you’ve let go of all that haunted you, maybe there’s no more spite to use as fuel anymore. Maybe there are only so many second chances. I’m a man of a thousand second chances and I’m all out of second chances. 

I’m still here. Still trying. And maybe, just maybe, that’s enough. Because if there’s one thing that I am confident about, if there’s one thing that I know for sure, even among all this chaos and confusion about who I am, if there’s one thing at all that is a constant in me, it’s this; I always pull through. It’s that I won’t be broken. I may bend, I may falter, but I will rise. Time and time again, I always pull through. 

I might be all out of second chances, but I’m not out of fight. I’m not out of resolve. There’s still something left in me, a flicker of determination that refuses to go out. I believe there’s a flicker in all of us. It’s small, sometimes barely noticeable, buried under the weight of failure, heartbreak and disappointment. But it’s there, waiting for you to notice it, to nurture it. 

Find that flicker. It’s easy to let it fade. To hang up the gloves, to convince yourself that you’re done but as long as you can feel that small, stubborn ember inside you, you have something to fight for.  Find that flicker. Turn that flicker into a flame because even when the well runs dry, the journey continues and it’s up to you to redefine what that journey looks like. The journey never really ends, not as long as there is a next day, it just shifts, twists, and asks you to dig deeper, to redefine what it looks like. You’re the one in power. You’re the one in charge. You get to choose what comes next. You get to decide how you’ll rise from the ashes of all those failed chances. You get to rewrite your story, even if the ink is smudged and the pages are torn. As long as there is such a thing as a next page, find a way to pull through. Because as long as there’s a flicker, there’s still hope. And there ain’t no fuel as strong as hope.

I’ve been called a lot of things. Stubborn. Cocky. Clever. Lazy. But one title I wear the best; I’m a man of a thousand second chances and I always pull through.
